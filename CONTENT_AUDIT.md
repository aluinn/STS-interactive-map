# Content audit

Snapshot of the data as it stands after the stabilisation pass (140 entries, 122
relationships, 18 debates, 24 guided paths, 7 knowledge routes). No new subject
areas were added during this pass.

## Strongly covered

- **Philosophy of science** – induction, verificationism, falsification, Duhem–Quine,
  paradigms, realism and anti-realism, experiment and models, values in science
  (about 29 entries, 18 debates).
- **Early modern astronomy and the "Scientific Revolution"** – Copernicus to Newton,
  with Babylonian, Islamic, Chinese and Jesuit-mediated context.
- **Sociology of scientific knowledge and practice** – SSK, the Strong Programme,
  laboratory ethnography, ANT, epistemic cultures, boundary-work.
- **Science, innovation and policy theory** – linear model, Mode 2, Triple Helix,
  mission-orientation, multi-level perspective, sociotechnical imaginaries.
- **Technology and politics** – Winner, SCOT, Collingridge dilemma, geoengineering.
- **Expertise and public participation (UK/Europe cases)** – Cumbrian sheep farmers,
  AIDS activism, HPV vaccine controversy.

## Partially covered

- **Colonialism and empire** – Bengal, Korea, Congo, Uganda cotton, scientific racism;
  little on South Asia beyond famine, the Caribbean/plantation sciences, Latin America,
  or Indigenous knowledge systems in their own right.
- **Global and local knowledge** – West Africa, East Africa, China, Islamic world are
  represented; South/Southeast Asia, the Americas and the Pacific are thin.
- **Gender** – Cavendish, Merian, Somerville, Franklin/Wu, Haraway, Martin, feminist
  epistemology; few entries on women in laboratories, computing or medicine.
- **Scientific labour** – technicians and instrument makers appear inside other entries
  (Watt, Leonardo, Tycho); no dedicated entries on invisible technicians, "computers",
  or precarious academic labour.
- **Open science / Big Science** – Bermuda Principles, Galaxy Zoo, open access,
  Manhattan Project, Weinberg; missing CERN-style collaborations, preprints and
  replication.
- **Trust** – vaccines and climate scepticism only.

## Missing

- Science communication and journalism; public understanding of science.
- Big Tech, platform and data-driven science (only AI and "enshittification" stubs).
- Medicine and public health beyond cholera (germ theory has Pasteur only).
- Twentieth-century physics and biology milestones (relativity, quantum theory, DNA
  structure as an event, the Modern Synthesis).
- Statistics, measurement and standardisation.
- Disability, race and science beyond "scientific racism".

## Duplicate or overlapping material

No duplicate IDs, slugs or relationships were found. Overlaps worth a future decision:

- `scholasticism` ("Scholastic Natural Philosophy") vs `natural-philosophy`
  ("Natural Philosophy") – different periods and arguments, but the titles read alike.
- `royal-society`, `scientific-societies` and `henry-oldenburg-philosophical-transactions`
  cover adjacent ground; they cross-link but repeat some framing.
- `science-policy-ecology` and the innovation-policy cluster (`linear-model`, `mode-2`,
  `triple-helix`, `mission-oriented-innovation`) share themes and could be tied together
  with explicit relationships.

## Needs source verification

- `trickle-down-science` – marked `sourceNeeded`, no sources listed.
- `platform-enshittification` – marked `sourceNeeded`; a journalistic coinage, needs
  academic or primary sourcing.
- `ai-governance-and-safety-institutions` – marked `sourceNeeded`; fast-moving, dates
  and institutions should be re-checked.
- Entries with `confidence: contested` (e.g. Thales, Pythagoras, House of Wisdom,
  Robert Moses) are deliberately hedged but their sources are single secondary works.
- 137 of 140 entries cite books or articles without a URL or DOI, so no link can be
  followed from the Sources panel. Adding DOIs where they exist is a good small task.

## Data that exists but is not shown in the interface

- The 7 `knowledgeRoutes` (Baghdad translation, Republic of Letters, Zheng He, Jesuit
  China exchange, Origin of Species translations, Congo extraction, M-Pesa) are defined
  and validated but no view renders them yet. Either add a Routes view or draw them as
  map layers before adding more.
- 49 entries are conceptual and have no single location. They now appear in the map's
  "ideas with no single place" list and in the Timeline and search.
- The `debate` entry kind and the `image` / `imageAlt` fields are defined in the types
  but unused.

## Suggested future additions (priority order)

1. A Routes view for the existing knowledge routes (no new research needed).
2. Source the three `sourceNeeded` entries or fold them into better-evidenced ones.
3. Science communication and public understanding of science.
4. Scientific labour: technicians, "human computers", precarious research work.
5. South/Southeast Asian, Latin American and Pacific knowledge traditions.
6. Big Tech, platforms and data-driven science.
7. Twentieth-century physics and biology milestones.
8. Medicine and public health beyond cholera.
9. Statistics, measurement and standardisation.
10. Add DOIs/URLs to existing sources.


## Update: time travel and expansion (October 2026)

The content expansion was started at the owner's explicit request. The dataset now has
226 entries (86 added), 164 relationships (42 added), 20 debates (2 added) and 29 guided
paths (5 added, 1 extended).

New strands: **Communication and Publics** (deficit/dialogue/participation models, publics,
expertise, trust, risk and uncertainty, media and meaning, museums and restitution, citizen
science, wicked problems), **Digital Power** (a non-inevitable history of computing, the
web, platforms, AI; elites, business models, knowledge infrastructure, news and
disinformation, regulation, the self), **Modern scientific geography** (Bay Area history,
Boston–Cambridge, London, Oxford–Cambridge, Geneva, Bangalore, Shenzhen, Hsinchu,
Singapore, Seoul, Tokyo, Nairobi, Cape Town and the Karoo) and a **cartography to Earth
observation** thread (Mercator, the Great Trigonometrical Survey, Earthrise, Landsat, the
Keeling Curve, the IPCC).

Still thin: South America and the Pacific; Indigenous knowledge systems; medicine and public
health beyond the cases above; twentieth-century physics and biology milestones; disability.

Entries marked `draft` rely on general scholarship rather than a full source check, and
`lastVerified` is set only where a claim was checked against live sources (EU digital
regulation, US v. Google, Meta fact-checking). No second-year material was added.
